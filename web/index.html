RigSettle

Hashrate rentals, settled by validators

You rented 10 GH/s. You got 6. Who decides what you owe?

With RigSettle the buyer's payment is locked in a GenLayer Intelligent Contract. The seller's rigs mine to the buyer's pool, so the pool's public worker page is a meter neither side can edit. When the rental ends, each validator reads that page and copies the per-slot numbers into a fixed form. Plain code, not a model, works out the delivered share and splits the escrow.

  1. LockThe buyer locks the price. Validators check the pool page can be read. The seller locks a bond.
  2. MineThe seller's rigs mine to the buyer's pool for the agreed window.
  3. SettleValidators copy the pool's numbers and must agree within 2 points. The seller gets the delivered share; the buyer gets the rest.

    How settlement works

    The model only copies numbers. Every decision below is plain Python in the contract, and validators must reach the same answer.

    A pool estimates hashrate from the shares it accepts, so a perfect rig shows a little noise; the grace band absorbs it. An outage excuses the seller only if the status site named in the terms reports it and the meter shows the drop in the same slots. A page that cannot be read moves nothing. Payouts are sent when the settlement transaction is finalized, after GenLayer's appeal window.
